      How to use
      
        
      
      
      
      
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
      
      
      
      

        
      
          
          
      This guide gets a process manager started with the Process Discovery Tracker (PDT). It follows one example, the demo project that comes with PDT: an order-to-cash process. The SME, Maria Keller, recorded four orders at a sales desk. For each order they found the customer in Order Desk, a web app, entered the order there and added it to the tracking sheet in Excel. The second order also needed a printed confirmation and a material looked up in Notepad, the third was for a new customer, and the fourth started on the wrong customer. The demo project is this example finished: open it to see what each act gives.
      
          
      What PDT does
      
          
        
            
      1. The SME records the process a few times: each pass is a transaction, for example one order.
        2. 
            
      2. PDT drafts the steps of each transaction and draws the process map.
        3. 
            
      3. You fix and name the steps of one transaction, the reference transaction, and confirm it.
        4. 
            
      4. You write the document fields, and PDT writes the PDD with the map: the process written down, for the SME to confirm and for whoever asks about it.
        5. 
            
      5. You hand the PDD and the whole project to the developers as a numbered version.
        6. 
          
      
          
      Start a project
      
          
        
            
      • From the SME's recording. The SME records with the capture app and sends you the zip it makes (Make a zip for the analyst). In PDT, choose Open, Open a zip... and pick the zip, then say where its folder is made (beside the zip unless you choose another place). PDT unzips it, opens it and analyzes the recording by itself: the top bar says Updating steps and map... until it is done. You can also drop the zip on the page shown while no project is open. The SME can answer four short questions and add example files when the recording stops: the answers wait under their fields on the Overview, the files are in Files.
        • 
            
      • From a video. Choose New, New project from a video... and pick the video, for example a Teams recording of the SME at work, with its transcript if you have one: pick both at once. Several videos go one after the other in the order of their names, and each transcript goes with the video whose name it shares. Then name the project: its folder is made where you save it, with a copy of the videos and the transcripts attached. It opens on the Timeline, where you mark the steps as you watch (Mark a video as you watch it, below).
        • 
            
      • From nothing. Choose New, New empty project... and name the project. You add the steps by hand, or paste your notes: in Steps, click Paste notes... and paste them, and each line becomes a step.
        • 
            
      • The demo. Choose Open, Open the demo project. A finished project opens: four orders entered in the Order Desk web app and an Excel tracking sheet, with its video, transcript, flow, sub-process, notes, attachments, document and maps. Each time you get a fresh copy in Documents\PDT demo, so change anything.
        • 
          
      
          
      Help, Recording keys, lists what to tell the SME before they record.
      
          
      Your first hour
      
          
      The Overview's Checklist lists the main acts below and ticks each one when it is done; its button takes you to the next. For a recording the acts are Mark the transactions, Confirm the reference transaction, Review the other transactions, Write the document and Hand off. For a project made from a video or from nothing they are Add the steps, Write the steps (a name of its own and a description for each step), Review the other transactions when it has two or more, Write the document and Hand off. On the Timeline, Steps and the Map, the top bar shows the next act, such as Next: write the steps (1 of 7), and a click on it opens the Checklist.
      
          
        
            
      1. Attach the video and the transcript. On the Overview, click Attach a video... in the Video row and pick the video. PDT opens the Timeline: pause the video at a moment you recognize, click Mark frame, then click the same moment on the timeline. The video is copied into the project folder. Attach the meeting transcript (a Teams .vtt or .docx) with Attach a transcript... in the Transcript row: the synced video places its sentences in time. Without a video, Sync the transcript... on the Timeline's Transcript lane places it: pick a sentence, then click where it was said.
        2. 
            
      2. Check the transactions. On the Timeline, each transaction is a band, from Transaction 1 to Transaction 4. If one is missing, right-click the timeline where it starts and choose Start a transaction here.... If one starts or ends in the wrong place, type its times in Steps or drag its band on the Timeline (see Everyday tasks). Without a video, the screenshots show the screen where the video would be: click the timeline, or press Left or Right, and the screenshot of that moment shows.
        3. 
            
      3. Name the steps of the reference transaction, or pick them from the library. In Steps, transaction 1 is the reference transaction. Where the recorder cut a step wrongly, split or merge it first (see Everyday tasks). Then click each step and type its name, the title of the selected step: the step that ends at Save order becomes Enter the order. The same drafted step in the other transactions takes the name too. Where the library has activities for the step's system, the Name field lists them: pick one instead of typing.
        4. 
            
      4. Write short descriptions. Description, under the step's name, is the text the PDD prints under the step. One or two sentences are enough, for example on Add the order to the tracking sheet: Type the new order as a row of the order tracking sheet (order, customer, item, quantity, unit price) and save the sheet. With a transcript, what the SME said during the step is right under it: Add copies a sentence into the description. Play, on the step's line, plays the step in the step panel, which lists every time it happened. In a window narrower than 1440 pixels the panel opens only with Play, or with the step's in 3 of 4 transactions on its line when it happened in two or more, in place of the list; Escape gives the list back. A project without a video has no Play: there in 3 of 4 transactions opens the panel. What the recorder saw is under Recorded. Information, under the description, lists what the step uses or enters: first the fields the recorder saw, marked Recorded, then your own lines. Add information adds a line: the information, an example and where it goes, such as Customer number, 10482, SAP, Sold-to party. The PDD names the information, never an example; the Technical details give the developers the examples too. Automation, under the Flow line, says what the automation will do with the step: The automation does it, Stays with a person, No longer needed or Not decided yet; the PDD lists the steps by it under its improvement and automation scope. Choose No longer needed for a step that should not be needed (rework, duplicate effort): Steps and the step panel tag it No longer needed. Done by, after the system on the step's line, names who does the step, a role or a person; left empty, it is the role the recording was made under.
        5. 
            
      5. Set the flow where it branches or loops. In transaction 2 the SME printed a confirmation: that customer wants one with every order. On that step, Print the order confirmation, click Only when... and type the customer asks for a printed confirmation. In transaction 4 the SME started on the wrong customer, cancelled and searched again. On Enter the order, click Decision..., pick Find the customer under Back to and type when: the wrong customer was selected. The demo's flow also goes from Find the customer to Create the new customer, a step transaction 3 did, when the search finds no account, and ends early after Enter the order when the customer's credit is blocked. The map draws them all.
        6. 
            
      6. Confirm the transaction. Click Confirm transaction (Ctrl+Enter). The steps you did not change are accepted as they are. Undo confirm, beside the Confirmed badge, takes it back. A transaction with no drafted step, such as one you added by hand, has nothing to confirm: it shows no Confirm transaction, and the Checklist ticks the act.
        7. 
            
      7. Look at how the other transactions differ. Open transactions 2, 3 and 4 in Steps. Their rows show how each differs from the reference transaction: Extra, Again or Moved on a step, and a greyed Skipped row where a step of the reference transaction is missing (point at one to read it whole). A missing step that has a condition reads Not done instead, which is no difference. The Checklist asks you to open only the transactions that differ. Transaction 2 has two extra steps: the printed confirmation and the Notepad lookup. Transaction 3 has one: Create the new customer. Transaction 4 has no differences, and a line says that part of it was left out of the analysis (the stretch where the SME started on the wrong customer). Where the recorder drafted the same work as another step, give it the name of the reference transaction's step: it then counts as that step. The other transactions are not confirmed.
        8. 
            
      8. Fill the document fields on the Overview. Open Document and write each field: title, purpose, scope, trigger, result, key contacts, business rules, exceptions, volume and effort, and the improvement and automation scope (the request and the notes are optional). For example, the title Order to cash: enter a customer order, the trigger A customer order arrives by e-mail and the volume and effort About 40 orders a day, 2 to 3 minutes each. The PDD prints each field in its own place, and the title names the files PDT makes: Order to cash enter a customer order PDD.docx (a file name cannot hold the colon). With a transcript, Add to puts a sentence into a field. Where a value is typed again in another system (here, the product from Order Desk in the tracking sheet in Excel), the PDD says so under the improvement scope.
        9. 
            
      9. Check the Map. The Map shows the map the PDD prints: a box per step with the system it is done in above it and who does it under it, a gear in its corner when the automation does it and a hand when it stays with a person, and the decisions from the flow you set. The demo's map starts at the trigger and ends at the result you wrote, with a decision before each step only some orders need, such as Create the new customer. Click a box to see each time the step happened, with its moment in the video and its screenshots. Show how the transactions ran shows the paths the transactions took; point at a line to see how often.
        10. 
            
      10. Hand off. Click Hand off... on the Checklist, or choose Export, Hand off.... PDT makes one zip with the whole project, the PDD and the process map (see Hand over to the developers). The PDD is written again from the project each time, so hand off again after a change. For the PDD alone, for the SME to read, choose Export, PDD (.docx). With attached files, as in the demo, the PDD comes as one zip with an Attachments folder: the attachment names in the PDD open the files once the zip is extracted, so the SME gets them with the document.
        11. 
          
      
          
      Everyday tasks
      
          
      Mark a video as you watch it
      
          
        
            
      1. Play the video on the Timeline: click Play, at the left of the row above the timeline, or press Space. To go to a moment, click the clock beside it (0:00 / 1:02:00), type a time and press Enter. The speed beside the clock plays the video at 1x, 1.5x or 2x. Where the SME starts the work, click Start transaction. While the video plays, the transaction starts at once; name it afterwards with Rename, on its header beside the video. Lanes, at the right of that row, chooses the lanes the timeline shows and says what their marks mean. To see a part of the timeline larger, click +: the bar under the lanes shows the whole length, and dragging its box moves along it. Double-click a transaction's band to show only that transaction; Show all shows the whole length again. With the keyboard, Tab goes to each lane of the timeline, the arrow keys go from mark to mark and Enter opens one.
        2. 
            
      2. As each step starts, press S (or click Add step), or right-click there and choose Add a step here, on the band of the step before it too. The step before it ends there. The newest step is open, fading out on the Timeline: it ends where you add the next one.
        3. 
            
      3. Where the transaction ends, click End transaction (the same button reads so while a transaction runs on): its last step ends there too. Start transaction for the next one ends the one before it the same way. Add note puts a note at that moment; notes stay out of the PDD. Right-clicking the timeline offers the same acts at the moment you click. A message about what you did reads in the same row.
        4. 
            
      4. Each step you add opens beside the video, in the Steps tab, its screenshot taken from the video at that moment. Pause the video before you press S, and the cursor is in the step's name: type the name, press Enter, and play on. Its system and its description are right under the name. While the video plays, S only marks the steps: name them in the Steps tab or in Steps afterwards. All steps goes back to the list. Ctrl+Z takes back a mark put in the wrong place, with its screenshot.
        5. 
            
      5. The Transcript tab beside the video lists what was said, with its time and who said it. Type in its search to find a word, for example invoice: it shows how many sentences hold it. With several speakers, pick one to read only theirs. The sentence the video is at is marked and stays in view while the video plays, unless your pointer is on the list or the search holds text. What is said also shows under the video, at the moment it is said: click it to find it in the Transcript tab.
        6. 
            
      6. Click a sentence to go to that moment. Add step here, after the sentence, adds a step that starts where it is said. Copy to... copies the sentence into the description of the selected step or into a field of the document.
        7. 
            
      7. Steps written first, from your notes, have no time: the Steps tab beside the video says how many, and each of them has Starts here on its row. Go to the moment a step starts in the video, then click Starts here on that step: the step starts at that moment and, without a picture, takes the video's frame there. Its place among the steps stays. Right-clicking the timeline offers the same at the moment you click, naming the step, and Ctrl+Z undoes it.
        8. 
          
      
          
      Put a step where it belongs in the process
      
          
        
            
      1. Late in the video the SME corrects a step shown earlier, or adds a condition or a step that belongs before: mark it where it is said, as any other step (press S, or right-click and choose Add a step here).
        2. 
            
      2. Right-click its band on the Timeline, or its row in Steps, and choose Place after a step..., then pick the step it comes after, in its transaction or in another one. In Steps, Alt+Up and Alt+Down move it one step at a time.
        3. 
            
      3. In the process, on the map and in the PDD it now comes after that step. On the Timeline its band stays where it was said, with its video, and is marked as placed elsewhere in the process; its row in Steps reads Placed.
        4. 
            
      4. Back to its time, in its menu, puts it where its time puts it again. Ctrl+Z undoes a move.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Work with several videos
      
          
        
            
      1. On the Overview, click Add a video... in the Video row and pick one or more videos: several go in the order of their names. In a project made from videos they go after the last one, back to back. In a recording you sync the first with two clicks, as when you attach one, and the others go after it; Sync video... on a video's band syncs that one again.
        2. 
            
      2. On the Timeline the Videos lane shows each video as a band. The timeline runs across them: a moment on it is the same moment however many videos there are. The video that shows the clock's moment plays, then the next one.
        3. 
            
      3. To trim a video, drag the start or the end of its band, or right-click it and choose Trim the start here or Trim the end here. In a project made from videos, the videos after it and what you marked on them move with it, and a trim that would cut away a step or a mark is refused and says what is there. Give its start back and Give its end back undo a trim, and so does Ctrl+Z.
        4. 
            
      4. Right-click a band to replace or remove that video (a video whose file is not found offers Find the video...). Removing a video takes what you marked on it with it, after asking. A transcript belongs to one video: Attach a transcript... asks which, and its sentences move with that video. Add a transcript... in the Transcript row attaches the next transcript; each has its own row on the Timeline, with Sync the transcript..., Replace the transcript... and Remove the transcript.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Add a step the recorder missed
      
          
        
            
      1. In Steps, select the step it comes after.
        2. 
            
      2. Click + Add step (or press N), type its name and click Add step.
        3. 
            
      3. Set its system: type it first on the line under the step's name. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the step.
        4. 
            
      4. With a video, you can instead pause the video at that moment on the Timeline and click Add step (or press S), or right-click the timeline there and choose Add a step here: the step gets that time. In the demo, Look up the SAP material in transaction 2 was added at its moment: the recorder saw no action there. Right-clicking a step on the Timeline also offers to open it in Steps, or to delete it (a recorded step is hidden).
        5. 
            
      5. To add many steps at once, click Paste notes... in Steps and paste your notes from Notepad or Teams: each line becomes a step, the text after its first colon its description, and a line that starts with a document field's name and a colon (Trigger:, Volume:) goes to that field. The line under the box says what it will make. One Ctrl+Z takes it all back.
        6. 
          
      
          
      Change when a transaction or a step starts or ends
      
          
        
            
      1. In Steps, open the transaction: Starts at and Ends at are under its name. Type a time the way the timeline shows it, for example 0:09 or 1:05.5, and press Enter. Up and Down change it by a second. An empty Ends at runs until the next transaction starts, or to the end. A time that cannot be taken is refused: the field shows its time again, and why stays under it until you change the field.
        2. 
            
      2. What you marked together moves together: a step that starts or ends where the transaction does moves with its Starts at or Ends at. A step you delete gives its time back to the step before it.
        3. 
            
      3. A step you added has its times on the line under its name, for example Transaction 1 · 0:09 to 0:31: type a time there and press Enter. Emptied, its end goes back to where the next step starts or its transaction ends. A step added without a time says no time yet: type when it starts. The step then belongs to the transaction its start is in, and a step right against it moves along. A step the recorder drafted keeps the time of its recorded actions: split or merge it instead.
        4. 
            
      4. On the Timeline you can instead drag the start or the end of a transaction's band, or of a step you added; a transaction that runs to the end gets its end that way too. Near a step's start or the clock's moment it snaps there. Esc while dragging puts it back, and Ctrl+Z undoes a change.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Split or merge steps
      
          
        
            
      1. Select the step in Steps. The toolbar above the list acts on it: Split..., Merge with next, Hide step, Open as a sub-process, and Delete on a step you added.
        2. 
            
      2. To split it, click Split... (or press S): Recorded, under the step, lists its actions, and you click the scissors after the action where the first part ends. Recorded stays open or closed as you last left it.
        3. 
            
      3. To merge it with the next step, click Merge with next (or press M). To merge several, tick them in the list and click Merge selected. To move the line between two steps, merge them, then click the scissors where the first should end: the place where they met is marked.
        4. 
            
      4. A merge is made in the other transactions too, and a name you then give the merged step reaches them, so the map keeps one box.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Add a screenshot, a note or a file
      
          
        
            
      1. Select the step in Steps.
        2. 
            
      2. Add screenshot: a frame of the video, a pasted image (Ctrl+V) or an image file. Use in PDD picks the one the PDD prints under the step.
        3. 
            
      3. Add note: notes stay in the app, beside the step on the Map; the PDD does not print them. In the demo, Add the order to the tracking sheet has one: Finance asked to keep the sheet until the weekly report can read Order Desk directly.
        4. 
            
      4. Attach file...: for example the material mapping file, on the demo's Look up the SAP material. It is copied into the project folder, the PDD lists it, and the PDD's export carries it. For a file of the whole project, such as the demo's order tracking sheet, use Add file... under the document fields on the Overview.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Leave out an app or a stretch of the recording
      
          
        
            
      1. An app, for example Calculator, where the SME checks a total out of habit: in Steps, click Apps outside the process..., tick the app and click Save.
        2. 
            
      2. A stretch, for example where the SME started on the wrong customer: on the Timeline, hold Shift and drag across it, click Leave out of the analysis in the row above the timeline, then Leave out.
        3. 
            
      3. The steps and the map are drafted again without it. A stretch left out shows hatched: click it and choose Include again to bring it back. Playing skips it, and the Transcript tab leaves out what was said in it.
        4. 
            
      4. In a project made from a video, a detour of the call, for example thirty minutes on another topic: hold Shift and drag across it, then click Not part of the process. Select a stretch: I where it starts, O where it ends, also while the video plays. It shows hatched, playing skips it, and the Transcript tab says Not part of the process in its place. Your steps stay as they are, and the maps and the PDD do not mention it. Go to a moment inside it to watch it anyway. Click it and choose Include again to bring it back, or press Ctrl+Z.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Make a sub-process
      
          
        
            
      1. To give a step its own steps, select it and click Open as a sub-process in the toolbar: Enter the order gets one step per field and click, from Enter Quantity to Click Save order.
        2. 
            
      2. To group steps, tick steps that follow one another, click Group into a sub-process... and name it, for example Find or create the customer.
        3. 
            
      3. Click ▸ 5 sub-steps on its row to open it; the line above the list goes back up. Dissolve sub-process undoes it.
        4. 
            
      4. In the PDD, each sub-process gets a chapter with its own map.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Edit the map as your As-Is map, and make a To-Be copy
      
          
        
            
      1. On the Map, click Edit as the As-Is map. The editor opens on a copy of the process map. Change it: the copy is kept once you change something.
        2. 
            
      2. Close the editor. The PDD now prints your As-Is map in place of the process map, and an analysis never changes it.
        3. 
            
      3. For the To-Be map, click Edit a new copy, set Use in PDD as to To-Be map and change the copy. The PDD prints it under the improvement scope.
        4. 
            
      4. Edited maps lists your copies, with Edit, Duplicate and Delete.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Open a second project in a new window
      
          
        
            
      1. Choose Open in a new window... in the Open menu, and pick the project folder.
        2. 
            
      2. It opens in a window of its own, and the project you had open stays open.
        3. 
            
      3. A project opens for editing in one window only: in a second window it opens read only.
        4. 
          
      
          
      Undo
      
          
        
            
      1. Press Ctrl+Z to undo your last change, and Ctrl+Y to redo it.
        2. 
            
      2. Undo and Redo in the top bar do the same.
        3. 
            
      3. Undo covers every change (steps, transactions, document fields, edited maps, the video, a stretch left out, a removed transcript), up to 50, until you open another project.
        4. 
            
      4. In the map editor, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo the changes to the map.
        5. 
          
      
          
      Hand over to the developers
      
          
        
            
      1. Choose Hand off... in the Export menu.
        2. 
            
      2. Type what changed in this version (for version 1 it says First hand-off already) and click Hand off.
        3. 
            
      3. PDT makes Order to cash enter a customer order v1.zip next to the project folder and opens its folder. Send that zip to the developers.
        4. 
          
      
          
      What a reader will miss. When the PDD of this version leaves something out, the dialog says so above the note: steps that are still drafts, steps with no name of their own, no description, no picture or no system, a decision that does not say when, empty document fields, or an As-Is map made before the steps changed. Click a line to go where it is fixed (nothing is handed off), or click Hand off anyway. Once everything before the hand-off is done, or after a version was handed off, the same list shows under the Checklist on the Overview: click A reader of the next version will miss to open it.
      
          
      What the zip holds. The whole project folder, without the backup copies of the working file and the analysis log. At its top: Start here.txt, which says what each file is; the PDD, Order to cash enter a customer order v1 PDD.docx; the Technical details, Order to cash enter a customer order v1 Technical details.xlsx; and the process map as a picture that opens in a browser (Order to cash enter a customer order v1 map.svg) and as BPMN 2.0 for ADONIS, Camunda, Bizagi and similar tools (Order to cash enter a customer order v1 map.bpmn). The Technical details is a spreadsheet for the developers: every click and entry of the reference transaction with the control it acted on, the fields typed in each transaction, the information each step uses or enters with an example, and the applications. A project made from a video or from nothing recorded none of that, so its zip has the Technical details only when its steps list information.
      
          
      How a developer opens it. In PDT, choose Open, Open a zip... and pick the zip. It opens read only: everything can be looked at and played, and nothing can change.
      
          
      The next version. Keep working in your project, then choose Hand off... again: PDT makes Order to cash enter a customer order v2.zip. The demo was handed off once already (its zip does not come with it), so Hand off... on it makes version 2. The PDD says which version it is and lists every version with what changed. Ctrl+Z takes a hand-off back; its zip stays.
      
          
      Where things are kept
      
          
        
            
      • The project folder holds everything: the recording, your work, the video, the transcript, screenshots, attached files, the library and your edited maps. Copy it whole, never in parts.
        • 
            
      • Every change you make saves itself to the project folder. The top bar says Saved; point at it to see the folder. If it says Not saved, point at it to see why.
        • 
            
      • The Export menu has three groups: For the developers and the archive (Hand off...), For another analyst (Whole project (.zip)) and Single files (the PDD, the Technical details and the map as an image or as BPMN).
        • 
            
      • Made on export: the PDD, the map (as an image or as BPMN) and the Technical details go where you save them. Whole project (.zip) and Hand off... make their zip next to the project folder. Every export opens its folder with the file selected.
        • 
            
      • Handing it on. To hand the project to another analyst, use Export, Whole project (.zip): one zip of the whole folder, made next to it. For the developers and the archive, use Hand off... instead.
        • 
          
      
          
      The library
      
          
      The library lists named activities for each system: for Order Desk, for example, Find the customer. Click the selected step's name: it lists the activities of the step's system, and a pick gives the step its name, its system and, when you wrote none, its description. You can still type any name. Where the library has no activities for the system yet, the field says so. A project keeps the library it started with, so a newer library does not change it. The owner of PDT maintains the library, and it comes with the app.
      
          
      When something looks wrong
      
          
      
            
      "This folder is not a project: it has no manifest.json."
      
            
      The folder holds no recording. Open the SME's zip itself with Open, Open a zip..., or open the recording folder inside the folder it was unzipped to.
      
            
      "This recording has no transactions marked."
      
            
      The SME did not press the transaction key. On the Timeline, right-click where each transaction starts and choose Start a transaction here....
      
            
      A step is listed under Outside the transactions.
      
            
      It starts before or after every transaction. In Steps, open the transaction and type an earlier Starts at. A step you added can instead be given a start inside the transaction.
      
            
      "The analysis failed, so the steps and the map are not drafted."
      
            
      The log at the top says why. Choose Help, Troubleshooting, Re-analyze: your confirmed, edited, added and hidden steps are kept.
      
            
      "The As-Is map was made before the steps changed: check it, or Copy again."
      
            
      On the Map, edit your As-Is map to match the steps, or click Copy again for a new copy of the process map as it is now.

      Transactions and drafted steps
      
          
      A transaction is one pass through the process, from where it starts to where it ends. The app drafts every transaction's steps: a step is one visit to a screen, and it ends at the action that commits the work there (Save, Search, Submit, Send) or where the work moves to another screen. Steps lists the transactions in time order; open one to see its steps. Steps outside every transaction are listed under Outside the transactions.
      
          
      Confirm the reference transaction
      
          
        
            
      1. Open the reference transaction: transaction 1, marked Reference, unless you choose another one (right-click a transaction, Use as reference transaction).
        2. 
            
      2. Check its steps against the screenshots or the video. Where a boundary is wrong, merge, split, add or hide steps (the toolbar above the list acts on the selected step; right-click a step for the same).
        3. 
            
      3. Name the steps the way the document should: double-click a name, or select the step and press Enter. In the step pane the name and the system are saved when you press Enter or leave the field, the description when you leave it; there is no Save button, and Ctrl+Z undoes each change. A step cannot have an empty name: the name it had comes back, and a line under it says so. A step's description is printed with it in the PDD.
        4. 
            
      4. Click Confirm transaction: the steps you did not change are accepted as they are. Undo confirm, beside the Confirmed badge, turns them back into drafts. A reference transaction with no drafted step (every step added or edited by you) has nothing to confirm and shows no Confirm transaction.
        5. 
          
      
          
      The step panel
      
          
      Click a box on the Map, or select a step in Steps: the step panel opens beside it. On the Timeline a step opens in the pane beside the video instead, in its Steps tab, with the fields of Steps. The panel shows every time the step happens in the transactions, each with its moment (▶ plays it in the panel's own video); on the Map also its name, system, description, screenshots, what was said and its notes. In Steps it shows only the video and those moments: the rest is in the editor beside it. ▶ Play, on the selected step's line in Steps, plays the step there and opens the panel again after you closed it. In a window narrower than 1440 pixels the panel opens in Steps only with ▶ Play, in place of the list of transactions, so the editor keeps its width; its close or Escape gives the list back.
      
          
      Fixes that carry to the other transactions
      
          
      A name you give a drafted step goes at once to the same drafted steps in the other transactions (each transaction says in one line how many of its names came that way). A merge in the reference transaction is also made in the other transactions wherever they have the same drafted steps one after the other, and one Ctrl+Z undoes all of it. Hiding a drafted step offers to hide it in every transaction. Splits and added steps are made in each transaction that needs them.
      
          
      Differences between transactions
      
          
      Every other transaction is compared with the reference transaction step by step, the way the PDD compares them: an open transaction lists its extra steps (and where they happen), its skipped steps, the steps done in a different order and the steps done more often than in the reference transaction, and its line counts them. A step you name like a step of the reference transaction counts as that step, whatever the recorder drafted. Said apart, above them: when part of it was left out of the analysis, which part, and a step that happens only under a condition (Only when) and was not done, which is no difference. The other transactions are not confirmed: once the reference transaction is confirmed and each other one has been opened, the Overview's Checklist ticks Review the other transactions.
      
          
      Sub-processes
      
          
      A step can hold steps of its own: a sub-process. Open as a sub-process, in the Steps toolbar, gives the selected step one step per action recorded in it (a field typed, a click; the cells of a sheet filled one after another are one step, and a step of one action has nothing to open); Group into a sub-process, in the Steps toolbar once two or more steps one after the other are ticked, moves them into a new one. Either is made in every transaction. The ▸ N sub-steps button on its row opens it, one block per transaction that goes through it, and the line above the list goes back up. Dissolve sub-process undoes it, and Ctrl+Z undoes that. Confirm transaction confirms every level.
      
          
      The flow of the steps
      
          
      After a step the next one follows. Where the process branches or loops, say so in the step pane's Flow line: Only when is for a step some transactions skip (the map draws a decision, with an "otherwise" path around it); Decision sends the process back to an earlier step (a loop), ahead to a later one, to a step another transaction did (the other side of a choice) or to its end, when a condition holds. For a choice between two steps, set the Decision on the step before them. Inside a sub-process the flow stays inside it: its end ends the sub-process.
      
          
      The activity library
      
          
      The step pane's Name offers the activities of the step's system from the activity library: pick one and the step takes its name and its system, and its description when you wrote none. You can still type any name. A project keeps the library it started with: its first analysis copies the app's library into the project folder as library.json. The app's library is the file library\activities.json in the app's resources folder; Build the library makes a new one from the names given in projects and hand-offs.
      
          
      
          
      What the step labels mean
      
          
      A step shows its label only where it differs from its transaction: the reference transaction's status (Draft or Confirmed) says it for the others, and a drafted step of another transaction shows none. Steps outside every transaction show theirs.
      
          
        
            
      • Draft drafted by the app and not checked yet; every analysis drafts it again.
        • 
            
      • Confirmed you accepted the draft as it is.
        • 
            
      • Edited you changed its name, its boundaries or its flags.
        • 
            
      • Added you added it by hand.
        • 
            
      • Hidden you took it out; Show hidden steps on its transaction lists it again.
        • 
          
      
          
      Every analysis keeps confirmed, edited, added and hidden steps as they are.
      
          
      Leaving things out
      
          
        
            
      • Apps outside the process, in the Steps toolbar: tick the apps that are not part of the process, for example Teams or Outlook used during the call; the steps and the map are updated without them.
        • 
            
      • Leave out of the analysis, on the Timeline: Shift-drag across a stretch (a break, a detour) and choose it in the row above the timeline, which then shows the stretch's times and what you can do with it. The steps and the map are drafted again without that stretch, and its time is not counted; a transaction with a stretch left out says so, and the reference transaction's steps done in it are not read as skipped. The Timeline shows the stretch hatched, Left out of the analysis: click it and choose Include again to bring it back. Ctrl+Z undoes either. Playing skips the stretch, and the Transcript tab leaves out what was said in it.
        • 
            
      • Not part of the process, the same on the Timeline of a project made from a video or from nothing: the stretch is put aside, hatched, skipped when playing and left out of the Transcript tab, and nothing else changes: the steps you marked in it stay, and the maps and the PDD do not mention it. Include again on it, or Ctrl+Z, brings it back.
        • 
            
      • Hide step (the Steps toolbar): takes one step out, and no analysis brings it back. Only a step you added can be deleted.
        • 
          
      
          
      What the automation does, and who does each step
      
          
      Automation, under the Flow line, says what the automation will do with the step: The automation does it, Stays with a person, No longer needed or Not decided yet; the PDD lists the steps by it under its improvement and automation scope. The process map puts a gear in the corner of a box the automation does and a hand in the corner of a box that stays with a person. Choose No longer needed for a step that should not be needed (rework, duplicate effort): Steps and the step panel tag it No longer needed, and the maps mark it: a dashed box on how the transactions ran, a Waste note beside its task on the process map. Done by, after the system on the step's line, names who does the step, a role or a person; left empty, it is the role the recording was made under. The process map says it under the box (once for the whole map when one role or person does every step), and the PDD on the step's line. Branches and exceptions are not flags: the map and the differences between the transactions show them.
      
        
      

        
      
          
      What the Map shows
      
          
      The Map shows the map the PDD prints: your As-Is map when you made one, else the process map the app draws from the reference transaction and the others, a box per step with the system it is done in above it and who does it under it (each said once when every step has the same one), a gear in its corner when the automation does it and a hand when it stays with a person, its notes and files beside it. In the map editor, a system or who does a step is text you can change: select it and type. The line above the map says which one shows. Show how the transactions ran draws the map from the transactions as they ran: hover a line for how often it was taken and its time; a dashed line is a path set in Steps that no transaction took. The PDD does not print it. Click a box for the step panel. Drag a map to move it; Ctrl and the mouse wheel zoom the process map, and the wheel alone zooms how the transactions ran. Fit fits the map in view, and Reset layout puts back the boxes you dragged.
      
          
      Sub-processes
      
          
      When a step holds a sub-process, Map: above the map chooses the main process or a sub-process; its number is its chapter in the PDD, and Steps shows the same level. On the process map, the arrow on a sub-process opens its map; on how the transactions ran, a double-click on a box marked + does.
      
          
      Your As-Is and To-Be maps
      
          
        
            
      • Edit as the As-Is map copies the process map into the editor, and the PDD prints the copy in its place. Change it there (Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo in the editor); it is kept once you change something, and analyzing the project again never changes it. When the steps change after it was made, the Map says so, with Copy again: a new copy of the process map as it is now, kept once you change something in it; the PDD then prints it in place of the old one, which stays in Edited maps.
        • 
            
      • Edit a new copy, once the As-Is map exists, starts another copy: choose To-Be map in Use in PDD as, and the PDD prints it under its improvement scope.
        • 
            
      • Edited maps lists your copies: Edit, Use in PDD as (None, As-Is map, To-Be map), Duplicate, Copy again and Delete (Ctrl+Z brings a copy back).
        • 
          
      
          
      Exporting the map
      
          
      Export, Process map (image) saves the map the Map shows as an SVG image, for Figma, Lucidchart or a browser. Process map (BPMN) saves it as a BPMN 2.0 file, with every sub-process, for ADONIS, Bizagi or Camunda. Both keep the steps' notes, which the PDD's pictures leave out.
      
        
      

        
      
          
      Transactions on the timeline
      
          
      A transaction runs from where it starts to where it ends; the SME marks both with the recording bar or the recording keys. Moving where a transaction starts or ends changes the transactions; the recording itself never changes.
      
          
        
            
      • Add: right-click the timeline at that moment and choose Start a transaction here or End the transaction here.
        • 
            
      • Move: in Steps, open the transaction and type its Starts at or Ends at; an empty Ends at takes its end out, and it runs until the next transaction starts. Or drag the start or the end of its band on the Timeline: near a step's start or the clock's moment it snaps there, and Esc puts it back. A step that starts or ends there moves with it. The steps follow by themselves a moment later.
        • 
            
      • Rename or delete: on the Timeline, in the Steps tab beside the video, each transaction's header has Rename and Delete transaction, with its Starts at and Ends at.
        • 
          
      
          
      To join two transactions, empty the Ends at of the first and click Delete transaction on the second. To split a transaction, start a transaction where its second part begins: a Start ends the transaction before it.

      The SME records with the capture app on their own computer. These keys mark the transactions while recording:
      
          
      
            
      Ctrl+Shift+SThe transaction key: start the next transaction (Transaction 1, Transaction 2, and so on). Ends the previous transaction first, and starts the recording if none is running.
      
            
      Ctrl+Shift+EEnd the current transaction.
      
            
      Ctrl+Shift+PTake a screenshot by hand.
      
          
      
          
      The recording bar on the SME's screen does the same without the keys: Start recording, then Next transaction for each transaction, Pause and Stop. End transaction is in the capture app's tray menu.
      
        
      

        
        
      
          
      Handing a version to the developers
      
          
      Each hand-off gets the next number, and the PDD says which version it is: its Document control reads Version 2, handed off with the date and your name, over a table of every version and what changed. A PDD exported between hand-offs reads Working copy after version 2.
      
          
      Opening a hand-off
      
          
      Open a zip... is also a button on the page shown while no project is open, and a hand-off zip dropped on that page opens the same way. The app keeps its own copy of the hand-off. A hand-off unzipped by hand opens read only too. The Overview's project line reads the version, the date and who handed it off; Export opens the PDD and, when the zip came with them, the Technical details. A newer version is a zip of its own, opened the same way.
      
          
      The Technical details
      
          
      A spreadsheet of what the recording holds for a developer: every click, entry and choice of the reference transaction with the control it acted on and how the recorder identified it, every field with what was entered in each transaction (a password was never recorded), the values typed again in another system, and the programs. Its Information sheet lists what each step uses or enters, with an example: the fields the recorder saw and the lines you typed under Information in the step editor. Export, Technical details (.xlsx) writes it on its own too. Where nothing was recorded and no step lists information (a project made from a video or from nothing) there are none: a hand-off goes out without them, and the export says so.

      Common issues
      
          
      
            
      A recording key did nothing
      
            
      Another program on the SME's computer may hold the same key. The capture app says so when it starts: Keys changed names the key it uses instead, Keys not available means there is none. Use the recording bar (Start recording, then Next transaction) or the tray menu (Next transaction, End transaction), or close that program and start the capture app again.
      

            
      A Citrix or remote desktop window shows as one app
      
            
      The recorder cannot see inside a remote window from the SME's computer, so the work done in it shows as one app with no steps inside. Run the capture app inside the remote session, or rely on the transactions the SME marks and a video for that part and add its steps by hand in Steps.
      

            
      The annotations for this project cannot be read
      
            
      A banner at the top says so, and nothing is saved while it shows. Restore the newest backup, on the banner, puts back the newest backup that can be read (it asks first and says when that backup was saved) and keeps the file that cannot be read beside it, as annotations.unreadable with the time.
      

            
      The steps or the map are missing or out of date
      
            
      The app analyzes a project by itself when it opens. If that stopped (it was cancelled, or it failed and the log at the top says why), analyze it again. Your confirmed, edited, added and hidden steps are kept.
